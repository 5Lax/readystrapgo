# ReadyStrapGo: three brand directions

October 16, 2026 · Identity concepts for review, not an approved identity or live storefront.

[Open the hero comparison](brand-preview.html) · [View the screenshot](brand-preview.png) · [Read the architecture proposal](ARCHITECTURE.md)

## The opportunity

ReadyStrapGo should make custom strap manufacturing feel tangible: show a strap, explain the choices, and help the buyer take the next step. Keep **ReadyStrapGo** as the brand name in all three directions, with **“Custom straps by A+ Products”** as the endorsement. The direction names below describe creative territories, not replacement business names.

The current [ReadyStrapGo homepage](https://readystrapgo.com/) uses a dark surface, orange highlights, a buckle icon, and an upload-first proposition. Its promises of instant quotes and automatic BOM extraction go beyond the connected workflows in the repository. The redesign should build confidence through real products, clear choices, and reliable follow-through.

[A+ Products](https://www.aplusproducts.net/) presents a broader manufacturer-facing hardware and sourcing business using black, white, red, and physical product photography. The [assembled webbing strap collection](https://www.aplusproducts.net/collections/assembled-webbing-straps) provides relevant product examples; the [custom assembly page](https://www.aplusproducts.net/pages/custom-assembled-webbing-straps) connects webbing, components, and construction. ReadyStrapGo can have its own identity while making that manufacturing relationship explicit.

Shared principles for all three directions:

- Show assembled straps first, then the component choices. Use the right A+ SKU images with source and usage approval recorded. A [hardware product detail](https://www.aplusproducts.net/products/fidlock-v-buckle-20-black-flap-with-pull-tab) is a reference for clear isolated photography; do not imply that its retail pack price is the price of a finished custom strap.
- Use “estimate,” “request a quote,” and “review your project” accurately. Do not invent fulfillment speeds, customer logos, certifications, load ratings, environmental claims, or minimum quantities.
- Offer three understandable starting points: customize an example, upload a specification, or discuss a sample. Explain what happens after submission.
- Preserve enough space for units, size, and compatibility information. The brand must work on a drawing, sample label, quote PDF, and staff screen as well as a homepage.
- Treat safety requirements as specifications to review, not as a visual promise. Outdoor imagery or sturdy-looking hardware does not establish a load rating.

## 01 — Precision Works

**One-line concept:** Clarity at every connection.

### Precision Works: positioning and buyer

A precise, organized partner for turning strap requirements into a buildable specification. Primary buyers are product engineers, industrial designers, sourcing managers, and procurement teams assembling bags, equipment, and manufactured goods. They want to identify the correct parts, communicate dimensions, and understand what is included in a quote.

The differentiator is legibility: clean product imagery, explicit dimensions, and a visible path from component selection to review. Choose this direction if repeat B2B production work is the main commercial goal. Avoid making the experience feel like an engineering exam; every technical term needs a plain-language explanation when first introduced.

### Precision Works: voice and sample copy

Direct, specific, calm. Short sentences and concrete nouns. Say “Choose a webbing width” and “This buckle fits these variants,” rather than promising limitless customization.

**Headline:** “Every part. One precise fit.”

**Subhead:** “Choose your webbing. Match your hardware. Bring a clear strap specification to the team that can make it.”

**Primary CTA:** “Configure your strap”  
**Secondary CTA:** “Upload a specification”  
**Supporting microcopy:** “Your estimate will list its assumptions. Our team reviews the details before production.”

### Precision Works: palette

| Role | Name | Hex | Intended use |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Primary | Cobalt | `#1746D1` | Primary actions, active controls, short headline emphasis |
| Secondary | Drawing ink | `#11233B` | Headings, navigation, technical footers |
| Accent | Signal yellow | `#DFFF00` | Small part markers, selected item accents, illustration details |
| Neutral | Drafting paper | `#F4F6F8` | Main surface |
| Neutral | White | `#FFFFFF` | Product cards, CTA text |
| Neutral | Slate | `#566176` | Body/supporting text |
| Neutral | Grid | `#D8DFE8` | Decorative rules and grids |

Calculated contrast: white on cobalt **7.41:1**; ink on drafting paper **14.60:1**; slate on drafting paper **5.76:1**; ink on signal yellow **13.89:1**. These pairs exceed 4.5:1 for normal text. Signal yellow should carry dark text, never white. Grid lines are decorative; use ink/cobalt for essential control edges and focus indicators. Show selection with text/checkmarks as well as color.

### Precision Works: font pairing

- [Space Grotesk](https://fonts.google.com/specimen/Space+Grotesk), 500–700: headlines and wordmark. Use compact spacing without collapsing counters; sentence case for approachable technical authority.
- [IBM Plex Sans](https://fonts.google.com/specimen/IBM+Plex+Sans), 400/500/600: body, labels, specification tables. Use tabular numerals for prices and measurements. Fallback: Arial, sans-serif.

### Precision Works: logo construction

Draw on a 32 × 32 unit grid. At x=5 and x=27, build two mirrored square brackets from y=5 to y=27, with 8-unit inward arms. Use a 3-unit stroke with square ends. Draw a horizontal bar from (5,16) to (27,16), then a filled 6 × 8 unit central block at (13,12). The result is an abstract joined buckle: two distinct parts aligned around one connection.

Place the mark left of the unpunctuated ReadyStrapGo wordmark in Space Grotesk Semibold. The mark is cobalt and the wordmark ink on pale surfaces; reverse both to white on ink. Keep clear space of half the mark width. Minimum mark size is 24 px; below that, use a simplified solid bracket silhouette. Do not add a checkmark that could imply certification. The SVG in the preview demonstrates the full mark, not a final trademark-cleared asset.

### Precision Works: homepage outline

1. Compact header: brand, Catalog, How it works, Resources, Sign in, and one “Configure your strap” action.
2. Hero: sample headline/subhead beside an exploded assembly photograph or precise schematic. Secondary spec-upload action.
3. Three starting assemblies, with intended application and available widths; each opens its actual detail page.
4. “Know what goes into it”: webbing, hardware, and construction cards with component closeups and labeled details.
5. Four-step process: select → configure → review estimate → submit for production review.
6. An annotated sample specification and BOM showing exactly what the buyer receives; clearly marked example data.
7. A+ Products manufacturing/support introduction and verified capabilities.
8. FAQ on minimums, samples, lead times, and estimate assumptions, using approved operational information.
9. Final “Bring us your specification” intake and a concise support/legal footer.

## 02 — Fieldwork

**One-line concept:** Made for what you make.

### Fieldwork: positioning and buyer

A material-minded manufacturing partner for people making things that move through everyday life. Primary buyers are outdoor and carry-goods designers, pet-product brands, and small product businesses choosing materials by feel, finish, and application as well as dimensions.

The differentiator is warmth and material literacy: inviting editorial layouts, close details of weave and stitch, and helpful explanations of tradeoffs. Choose this direction if sample conversations and design assistance drive sales. Keep technical specifications close to the imagery so the identity does not become generic outdoor lifestyle branding. Earth tones are a visual choice, not a sustainability claim.

### Fieldwork: voice and sample copy

Warm, practical, observant. Talk about the buyer's product before talking about the factory. Use “Let's work through the details” and “Start with the strap your product needs.” Avoid exaggerated adventure or indestructibility language.

**Headline:** “A small strap. A world of possibility.”

**Subhead:** “For the bag, the collar, the next great idea. Find the webbing and hardware that bring your product together.”

**Primary CTA:** “Find your starting point”  
**Secondary CTA:** “Talk through a sample”  
**Supporting microcopy:** “Have something in hand? Send a photo and tell us what you would change.”

### Fieldwork: palette

| Role | Name | Hex | Intended use |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Primary | Pine | `#173F35` | Wordmark, headings, dark panels |
| Secondary | Fired clay | `#B84A2B` | Main CTA and short editorial highlights |
| Accent | Ochre | `#E8BA72` | Material tags, illustration details, small background discs |
| Neutral | Canvas | `#F6F1E7` | Main warm surface |
| Neutral | Flax | `#E5DDCC` | Quiet photo mats, dividers |
| Neutral | Moss gray | `#536358` | Body and supporting copy |
| Neutral | White | `#FFFFFF` | CTA text, product cutout surfaces |

Calculated contrast: pine on canvas **10.37:1**; white on fired clay **5.18:1**; moss gray on canvas **5.66:1**; pine on ochre **6.50:1**. Use pine text on ochre and flax. Ochre is too light for body text on canvas. Keep textures behind illustrations rather than text, and use solid pine outlines for keyboard focus.

### Fieldwork: font pairing

- [DM Serif Display](https://fonts.google.com/specimen/DM+Serif+Display), regular and italic: large editorial headlines. Limit italics to a short phrase; do not use the serif for dense specifications.
- [DM Sans](https://fonts.google.com/specimen/DM+Sans), 400/500/600/700: body, controls, and wordmark. Fallbacks: Georgia for display; Arial, sans-serif for body.

### Fieldwork: logo construction

On a 32 × 32 grid, draw a rounded, almost closed strap loop with a 5-unit pine stroke: begin at (22,5), travel left to (14,5), curve through (5,14), continue to (5,18), curve through (14,27), continue to (18,27), then curve to (27,18) and finish at (27,14). Round the exposed stroke ends. Inside the loop, draw two fine diagonal stitches connecting (13,11) to (21,21) and (13,21) to (21,11). At the upper-right opening, add a 3-unit clay corner from (24,4) to (24,12) to (31,12).

The mark suggests a folded webbing loop with a stitched center and a small hardware connection. Pair with ReadyStrapGo in DM Sans Bold. Retain the open corner in a one-color version; use pine or white only on labels and embroidery. At small sizes, drop the diagonal stitches. Clear space is half the mark width; minimum detailed size is 28 px. This is an original abstract construction, not a tracing of a supplier buckle.

### Fieldwork: homepage outline

1. Quiet header: brand, Straps, Materials, Our process, and “Start a project.”
2. Hero: sample headline/subhead with one large finished strap, warm background, visible weave/stitch detail.
3. “What are you making?” application tiles: carry goods, pet products, outdoor gear, custom project.
4. Material notebook: nylon/polyester/other approved options with factual feel, finish, and use notes; link to full specifications.
5. Hardware pairings: two or three real closures on the same strap, explaining the design tradeoff.
6. “From your idea to a sample”: conversation → specification → sample review → production quote.
7. A+ Products people/process story with approved workshop imagery and genuine captions.
8. Sample and reference-photo intake: explain how to document a sample and get the correct mailing instructions.
9. Selected projects only when customer permissions exist; otherwise use clearly labeled example assemblies.
10. Practical FAQ, a human contact invitation, and footer.

## 03 — Open Studio

**One-line concept:** Bring your own possibility.

### Open Studio: positioning and buyer

An inviting creative workspace for assembling a distinctive strap from understandable choices. Primary buyers are emerging accessories brands, creative directors, branded-merchandise teams, and designers exploring color and hardware combinations before a manufacturing conversation.

The differentiator is visible creative range: modular component cards, bold color combinations, and a template-led entry into customization. Choose this direction if attracting new design-led brands is the priority. Keep the actual configurator calm and systematic; the homepage can be expressive without making a quote or specification feel like a game.

### Open Studio: voice and sample copy

Energetic, inclusive, lightly playful. Make the next action obvious. Say “Add your twist” and “Try another combination,” while keeping dimensional and commercial copy literal. No faux instant production or AI magic.

**Headline:** “Big idea? Strap it together.”

**Subhead:** “Mix webbing, color, and clever hardware. Start with a template and shape a strap that feels like your brand.”

**Primary CTA:** “Start creating”  
**Secondary CTA:** “See the possibilities”  
**Supporting microcopy:** “Save your combination. Share the details. We'll help you take the next step.”

### Open Studio: palette

| Role | Name | Hex | Intended use |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Primary | Studio violet | `#5425A8` | Wordmark band, headline emphasis, selected controls |
| Secondary | Peach | `#F1C9BD` | Editorial panels, component-card backgrounds |
| Accent | Electric lime | `#D5F36B` | Main CTA and small creative details |
| Neutral | Aubergine ink | `#251A34` | Headings, body, button text and outlines |
| Neutral | Lilac paper | `#F9F5FF` | Main surface |
| Neutral | Muted plum | `#665B73` | Supporting copy |
| Neutral | White | `#FFFFFF` | Text on violet, image cards |

Calculated contrast: white on violet **9.52:1**; ink on lilac paper **15.31:1**; ink on lime **13.21:1**; muted plum on lilac paper **5.90:1**; ink on peach **10.84:1**. Peach/lime are surfaces, not body-text colors on white. Use a dark outline around lime buttons so the control boundary remains clear. Selection and errors need labels/icons beyond hue.

### Open Studio: font pairing

- [Bricolage Grotesque](https://fonts.google.com/specimen/Bricolage+Grotesque), 600–800: expressive display headlines and a compact wordmark. Keep exaggerated weight in marketing, not the BOM.
- [Manrope](https://fonts.google.com/specimen/Manrope), 400/500/600/700: body and product UI. Use tabular numbers for specifications and prices. Fallback: Arial, sans-serif.

### Open Studio: logo construction

On a 32 × 32 grid, draw a 19 × 12 unit capsule at (3,10) with 6-unit corner radii and a 4-unit stroke. Add an open chevron from (19,5) through (29,16) to (19,27), also 4 units thick, with rounded joins and ends. The capsule is a soft loop of webbing; the chevron reads as a forward connection and “Go.” They overlap slightly to feel assembled rather than merely adjacent.

Use lime on violet for the primary mark, with a white ReadyStrapGo wordmark in Bricolage Grotesque Bold. On white or peach, render both in violet or ink. The one-color version retains both outlines and the open center; do not add tiny stitching. Minimum mark size is 24 px; clear space is half the mark width. Check distinctiveness and trademark availability before final identity production.

### Open Studio: homepage outline

1. Compact violet header: brand, Templates, Parts, How it works, and “Start creating.”
2. Hero: sample copy with an oversized strap crossing separate color/material cards.
3. “Pick a starting point”: easy-to-scan templates with actual finished assembly previews.
4. “Make it yours”: three controlled demonstrations of color, width, and closure choices; no fabricated configurator functionality.
5. A small component gallery: webbing, buckles, rings, and adjusters with plain-language names and real SKU links.
6. How it works: start a template → confirm details → send for review, with save/resume explained.
7. “Made with A+ Products”: the manufacturing relationship, process photographs, and approved facts.
8. Community/project inspiration with permissioned work, or clearly marked internal concept studies.
9. FAQ on samples, pricing review, and custom branding, then a final “Start creating” invitation and footer.

## Recommendation and selection criteria

Start with **Precision Works** if ReadyStrapGo's primary job is to convert a manufacturer's requirement into a repeatable production request. Its visual order fits the proposed guided configurator, BOM, and reviewed quote system. Fieldwork is the strongest alternative for a relationship-led business where physical samples and material guidance drive conversion. Open Studio is the most expressive option for attracting emerging brands.

| Direction | Primary promise | Best proof | Main risk to test |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Precision Works | Clear choices and specifications | Accurate components, annotated BOM, dependable review | Looks intimidating to a first-time buyer |
| Fieldwork | Material understanding and human support | Macro photography, useful comparisons, sample process | Looks like a lifestyle retailer rather than a custom manufacturer |
| Open Studio | Room to create a distinctive product | Template variations, real component choices, saved concepts | Playfulness reduces confidence in production/commercial detail |

Test each with representative buyers using the same tasks: identify the product/service, find a relevant strap, explain what happens after the CTA, and locate technical details. Select one primary system and carry it through customer and staff workflows. Do not mix the three palettes or display typefaces into one identity. Final logo artwork, legal clearance, photography selection, and production design tokens remain future design work.
